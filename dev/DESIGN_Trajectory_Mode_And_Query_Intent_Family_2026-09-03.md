## THE FINDING
The 9/3 reading exposed a real query-intent class the engine already half-serves: future-shaped questions asked as PROJECT MANAGEMENT, not prophecy. **Future-shaped language does not require predictive use.** The actual question under "could this eventually provide?" is: given the present configuration, does the current vector support the named outcome — what supports it, what obstructs it, what is writable now?

## THE TRAJECTORY CONTRACT (Air's line, candidate law)
> **The field can judge whether the present vector supports the proposition. It cannot guarantee that the proposition becomes actuality.**
Verdicts (YES / YES,BUT / NO,BUT / NO) mean "does the present field support the proposition as things currently stand" — never "has the future been revealed."

## THE QUERY-INTENT FAMILY (interpretive layer, NOT new metaphysics)
- **MIRROR** — what am I seeing right now?
- **TRAJECTORY** — given where things stand, does this path currently point toward X?
- **DECISION** — what changes in me/the field between A and B? (dovetails the existing Choice-reading design)
- **PRACTICE** — what is mine to do with what I am seeing?
**Implementation law:** infer intent from ordinary language when confidence is high; never force a mode selector on a human question. ## REGISTER DISCIPLINE (the four repairs — candidates for a VECTOR-NOT-PROPHECY prompt law)
From the 9/3 export, the structural signal was right; the prose overreached in four ways. The law in one line: **claim the vector, never the arrival.**
1. Never assert the hard part is "done" — the draw establishes something real EXISTS, not that the remaining road is short.
2. Never estimate distance in lived time/effort ("not a long road") — the geometry has no odometer.
3. Never place the outcome deterministically behind one move. Say: "the next part of the question lives in that move." **Exchange produces information before it necessarily produces provision.**
4. No promise-shaped folk wisdom ("given things have a way of returning"). Say: "only circulation can reveal what the world is willing to return for what has been built."
